# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue about code, not about people. Assume the person on the other
side is trying to make the project better.

## Why this document exists

Secretly is used by people for whom a leaked conversation is not an
embarrassment but a danger — journalists, human rights defenders, people living
under occupation or under war. Some of them will one day read this repository,
and a few will contribute to it. The tone here is part of what they are being
asked to trust.

## You may contribute anonymously

You are not required to use your legal name, disclose your country, your
employer, or anything else about yourself. A pseudonym and an email address are
enough. Nobody will be asked to prove who they are in order to have a patch
reviewed.

This is deliberate. A privacy project that demands identification from its
contributors is not a privacy project.

The one exception is the contributor agreement ([CLA.md](CLA.md)), which needs a
name you are prepared to stand behind. A consistent pseudonym is acceptable
there too.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on the work, with a reason attached.
- Accept that a change may be declined, and that "this is out of scope" is a
  complete answer.
- Say when you do not know something. In cryptography this is the most valuable
  thing anyone says.
- Respect a maintainer's silence during war. Replies may take days. They are not
  being ignored.

## What is not acceptable

- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning comments.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private, including unwanted contact after
  being asked to stop.
- Publishing anyone's private information — a real name, an address, a
  workplace, an IP address — without their explicit permission. In this project
  that is not a social faux pas; it can put someone in physical danger.
- Sexual attention or imagery.
- Deliberately introducing a weakness, a backdoor, or a dependency you know to
  be compromised. This ends participation permanently and will be disclosed
  publicly.
- Pressing a maintainer to disclose an unfixed vulnerability before its
  coordinated date, or using an unfixed report as leverage.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, commit messages — and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Email **support@secretlyapp.com**. If the problem involves a maintainer, say so;
it will still be read by a human who will answer.

Tell us what happened and what you would like to see done. You will get an
acknowledgement within 3 working days. Your report will not be shared beyond
the people who must act on it, and never with the person reported, unless you
ask for that.

If reporting by email is itself a risk to you, open an issue saying only that
you need a private channel, and we will arrange one.

## What happens next

Depending on what occurred: a private word, a public correction, a temporary
block, or a permanent ban. We will tell you what was decided. We will not
pretend that a response was stronger than it was.

We are a very small project. There is no committee and no appeals board. What
there is instead is a commitment to answer honestly and to explain the reason
for whatever is decided.
